Topic: Matrix – Orthogonal Matrices | Level: 12th / Diploma

Q1: If A and B are orthogonal matrices and det(A)+det(B)=0, prove that A+B is singular.

Answer: A+B is singular (click to view explanation)

Orthogonal matrices have determinant ±1. The condition forces one determinant +1 and the other −1. Consider B⁻¹A; its determinant is det(A)/det(B)=−1. An orthogonal matrix with determinant −1 has eigenvalue −1, so there exists x≠0 with B⁻¹A·x=−x. Multiply by B to get A·x=−B·x, hence (A+B)x=0 for nonzero x. So A+B is singular.
Formula: det(AB)=det(A)det(B)

Q2: If a 2×2 matrix has eigenvalues 3 and 4, find det(A).

Answer: 12 (click to view explanation)

For any square matrix, the determinant equals the product of its eigenvalues. So det(A)=3×4=12.
Formula: det(A)=∏λᵢ
Topic: Limits | Level: 11th

Q3: Evaluate lim(x→0)(sin3x/x).

Answer: 3 (click to view explanation)

Use lim(t→0)(sin t / t)=1. Put t=3x, so sin3x/x=(sin3x)/(3x)×3→3.
Formula: lim(t→0)(sin t / t)=1
Topic: Differentiation | Level: 11th

Q4: Find d/dx(x³ sinx).

Answer: 3x² sinx + x³ cosx (click to view explanation)

Use product rule: (uv)'=u'v+uv'. Here u=x³, v=sinx, so derivative=3x²sinx + x³cosx.
Formula: (uv)'=u'v+uv'
Topic: Coordinate Geometry — Circle Tangent | Level: 11th

Q5: Length of tangent from (2,3) to circle x²+y²=5?

Answer: 2√2 (click to view explanation)

Tangent length formula: √(x₁²+y₁²−r²). Here x₁²+y₁²=13 and r²=5, so √(13−5)=√8=2√2.
Formula: √(x₁²+y₁²−r²)
Topic: Partial Differentiation | Level: 12th

Q6: Find stationary point of f(x,y)=x²+2y²−4x+8y.

Answer: (2,−2) (click to view explanation)

Set partial derivatives to zero: fx=2x−4=0⇒x=2, fy=4y+8=0⇒y=−2. Hessian diagonal (2,4) positive ⇒ local minimum at (2,−2).
Formula: fx=fy=0 for critical point
Topic: Vector Dot Product | Level: 11th

Q7: Compute a·b for a=(1,2,3), b=(2,−1,1).

Answer: 3 (click to view explanation)

Dot product = sum of component-wise products: 1×2 + 2×(−1)+3×1=3.
Formula: a·b=Σaᵢbᵢ
Topic: Combinations / Probability | Level: 11th

Q8: From 5 men and 4 women choose committee of 3 with at least one woman. How many ways?

Answer: 74 ways (click to view explanation)

Total ways C(9,3)=84. All-male committees C(5,3)=10. So at least one woman: 84−10=74.
Formula: C(n,r)=n!/[r!(n−r)!]
Topic: Quadratic — Vieta | Level: 10th

Q9: For x²−5x+6=0, find sum and product of roots.

Answer: α+β=5, αβ=6 (click to view explanation)

For ax²+bx+c=0, sum=−b/a, product=c/a. So sum=5, product=6.
Formula: Vieta’s relations
Topic: Sequences — Sum of Naturals | Level: 10th

Q10: Sum of first n natural numbers?

Answer: n(n+1)/2 (click to view explanation)

Pairing method: (1+n),(2+n−1),... gives n/2 pairs each sum n+1. So Sn=n(n+1)/2.
Formula: Sn=n(n+1)/2
